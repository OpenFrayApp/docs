**Publishing** puts an encounter, or a single creature, at a link anyone can open. They
read it in their browser, and add it to their own board with one click. It is how you
hand a fight to another Game Master, put one under a blog post, or send a homebrew
creature to the person who asked for it.

This is not the [player view](/docs/guides/player-view/), which is a live screen for your
own table during a fight. A published link is prep: it carries no hit points, no effects,
and nothing about a fight in progress.

Publishing needs an account. The console doesn't, and that hasn't changed. Publishing puts
something of yours at a public address, so we ask you to sign in first: a published page
has somebody standing behind it, and you can take it down again.

## Publishing an encounter

The **share** button is under the tracker, at the foot of the column, beside the save
button. It's grayed out until there's a creature on the board to publish: a board holding
only your player characters has nothing to hand over.

1. Put the creatures you want on the board.
2. Click the **share** button.
3. Give it a **Name**. This is what a reader sees first.
4. Write a **Note**, if you want one. This is the place for how the ambush opens or what
   the boss does first. It takes basic formatting: bold, italic, quotes, lists, and
   headings.
5. Check the **byline**. It starts as the name on your account and you can type over it.
6. Choose an **Encounter license**, covered below.
7. Read the line above the button. It says what pressing it confirms: that you hold the
   rights to the encounter, and that you agree to the
   [terms](https://openfray.app/terms).
8. Click **Publish**.

The dialog then becomes the link itself, with the same three buttons the **Shared links**
page carries (**Copy**, **Open**, and **Unpublish**), and **Done** to close it.
**Unpublish** asks first, and taking it down here drops the dialog back to the form: the
code is gone for good, and publishing again makes a new link rather than reviving the old
one.

A **?** beside a field explains what it's for. Point at it, or tap it on a touchscreen.

What travels is the cast, the name, the note, the byline and the license. What does not:
your player characters, hit points, effects, initiative, the log, or anything else about
the fight you were running. The creatures are published as they are in the compendium, not
as they stand on your board.

:::caution[A note is public]
Anyone with the link reads it. Don't put anything in a note you wouldn't put on a web
page: no passwords, no real names, no addresses.
:::

## Publishing a creature

Open the creature in the compendium and click the **share** button on its stat block. The
dialog is the same, minus the encounter's name and license: a creature already carries its
own.

A creature brought in with the **OpenFray Importer** can't be published. Those stat blocks
are Wizards of the Coast's, and putting one on a public page isn't ours to do. It stays
yours to use at your own table.

## Licenses, and what they cover

Two different claims, and the split is the point.

**The encounter's license** covers your own words: the name, the note, and which creatures
you put together. It says nothing about the creatures themselves.

**Each creature's license** is whatever its author gave it, and it travels with the
creature. A creature from the SRD carries the SRD's terms. One you wrote carries whatever
you said when you made it.

Stating no license is the default, and it is not a grant: copyright reserves everything
unless somebody says otherwise. A creature with no license stated can be read on the page
but will not copy to anybody's library.

Your account can hold a **default license** that the dialog starts on. It's in
**Account**, and changing it changes nothing you have already published.

:::note[Publishing is a claim about your rights]
You confirm you hold the rights to what you publish. A creature you wrote is yours to
share. One you typed in out of a book you bought is not.
:::

## What a reader can do

Somebody opening your link gets a page with the encounter's name, your note, your byline,
and the cast. They can:

- read every creature's stat block;
- click **Use this encounter** to put the cast on their own board, which opens the console
  with the creatures staged;
- report the page, if it shouldn't be up.

Creatures that aren't licensed for reuse are read-only. They stay on the page and are left
behind when somebody adds it, and the app says which ones and why before it adds anything.

A shared link is unlisted, not secret. There is no directory of them and search engines are
told not to index them, but anyone you send the link to can send it on.

## Your published links

Everything you have published is listed in one place: click your **account**, then
**Shared links**.

Each row shows the name, whether it is an encounter or a creature, when you published it,
and the link itself. Beside each one:

- **Copy** puts the link on your clipboard again.
- **Open** shows you what a reader sees.
- **Unpublish** takes it down.

**Unpublishing is final.** The link stops working for everybody, immediately and for good.
Anyone who already added the encounter to their board keeps it, because it was copied onto
theirs when they added it.

Nothing expires on its own. A link you publish today still works next year unless you take
it down. An account can hold up to 200 published pages at once; unpublishing makes room.

Deleting your account takes down everything you published with it.

## Reporting a page

Every published page has a **Report this** button. Anyone can use it, and nobody is asked
who they are. A report says what is wrong (spam, sexual content, hate or harassment,
impersonation, something copied out of a book, or something else). It can carry a message
too, and an address if you want a reply.

Reports are read by a person. A page that breaks the [terms](https://openfray.app/terms) is
removed, and whoever published it is told at the address on their account. If you'd rather
write than use the form, the address is
[reports@openfray.app](mailto:reports@openfray.app).

If a page you published is removed, the link answers by saying so, rather than pretending
the code never existed.
